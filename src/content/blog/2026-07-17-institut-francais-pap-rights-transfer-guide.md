<p>The <a href="https://www.institutfrancais.com/en/programme/project-support/publication-assistance-program-pap-transfer-rights">Institut francais PAP transfer-of-rights page</a> is most useful when a foreign publisher reads it as a workflow document, not as generic French publishing encouragement. As of <strong>Monday, July 20, 2026</strong>, Institut francais says the 2026 window <strong>opened on June 5, 2026</strong>, <strong>closes on September 8, 2026</strong>, and goes to a <strong>selection committee on October 1, 2026</strong>.</p>

<p>That puts the real pressure on readiness. Rex readers do not need another broad reminder that French titles travel internationally. They need to know whether a rights deal, translator plan, and local filing route are organized enough to fit a grant system that is explicit about timing, exclusions, and market fit.</p>

<h2 class="mt-10 mb-4 text-2xl font-bold text-gray-900">The filing route is part of the eligibility story</h2>

<p>Institut francais says the programme is aimed at <strong>foreign publishing houses</strong> and that applicants should <strong>contact the establishments of the French cultural network in their country</strong> to submit the application. That matters because this is not just a passive web form waiting for any export-curious publisher to upload a file at the last minute.</p>

<p>In practice, a team should assume the local French cultural network needs enough time to review the project, understand the publisher profile, and form the opinion that the commission later uses. If your schedule assumes all judgment starts on September 8, it is already too loose.</p>

<h2 class="mt-10 mb-4 text-2xl font-bold text-gray-900">The exclusion list should save time before anyone builds the packet</h2>

<p>Institut francais says PAP covers all areas of publication <strong>except textbooks, technical works, and practical books</strong>. That line deserves more attention than the headline aid figures because it tells foreign publishers when <em>not</em> to spend time on this route.</p>

<p>For Rex readers, the practical lesson is simple: if the project sits in a strongly instructional or technical lane, do not assume PAP is the flexible answer. This programme is narrower than that, and treating it as universal French export support is a good way to waste both internal time and network goodwill.</p>

<h2 class="mt-10 mb-4 text-2xl font-bold text-gray-900">Selection turns on market judgment, not only literary enthusiasm</h2>

<p>The same page says the commission relies on the cultural network's detailed opinion and then weighs several factors: the publisher's risk-taking, the quality and importance of the work, the relevance of the translation in the local market, the quality of the translation and translator references, the amount requested, and the publisher profile.</p>

<p>That is useful because it shows the real standard. PAP is not only asking whether a French-language work is worthy. It is asking whether the proposed translation belongs in a specific market, through a publisher that can make credible use of it. A house that cannot explain local fit, translator quality, and publishing rationale is not ready just because it likes the title.</p>

<h2 class="mt-10 mb-4 text-2xl font-bold text-gray-900">The 2025 numbers are context, not a promise</h2>

<p>Institut francais says PAP and related programmes have supported nearly <strong>26,000 titles in 80 countries since 1990</strong>. On the same live page, it reports that the <strong>2025</strong> cycle saw <strong>509 submitted projects</strong>, <strong>285 selected projects</strong>, <strong>52 countries supported</strong>, and <strong>378,557 euros</strong> granted.</p>

<p>Those numbers are best read as competition context. They show that PAP is active and substantial, but they do not mean a prepared file can ignore category fit or local-market logic. For smaller publishers, the safer reading is that a real programme exists, and so does real selectivity.</p>

<h2 class="mt-10 mb-4 text-2xl font-bold text-gray-900">Do not confuse PAP with the separate CNL route</h2>

<p>The <a href="https://www.institutfrancais.com/en/books-and-publishing">Institut francais books-and-publishing overview</a> places PAP inside a broader French export-support system, and the <a href="https://www.booksfromfrance.fr/grants?grant_type_id=2&amp;locale=en">Books From France grants directory</a> makes clear that foreign publishers may also encounter a separate CNL translation-support path. Those are not the same workflow.</p>

<p>That distinction matters operationally. PAP is routed through the French cultural network in-country. The CNL directory describes a different support mechanism tied to the French publisher that sold rights. If a team collapses those into one imagined grant lane, it will likely misfile or mis-sequence the project.</p>

<h2 class="mt-10 mb-4 text-2xl font-bold text-gray-900">What to do before September 8, 2026</h2>

<ul class="mb-6 list-disc pl-6 space-y-2">
  <li>Work from the actual 2026 closing date: <strong>September 8, 2026</strong>.</li>
  <li>Contact the relevant French cultural-network office early enough for a real review cycle.</li>
  <li>Screen out projects that fall into <strong>textbooks</strong>, <strong>technical works</strong>, or <strong>practical books</strong>.</li>
  <li>Build a case for local-market fit, translator quality, and publisher credibility, not just title enthusiasm.</li>
  <li>Keep PAP separate from any CNL translation-aid plan so the filing route stays clean.</li>
</ul>

<p>The useful takeaway is blunt. Institut francais PAP is a live 2026 rights-support workflow, but it works best when a foreign publisher treats <strong>September 8, 2026</strong> as a network-routed deadline with real category limits, not as a generic late-stage funding option for any French title already drifting into the schedule.</p>
