<p>The Polish Book Institute's <a href="https://www.bookinstitute.pl/programy/the-poland-translation-programme/">live ©POLAND Translation Programme page</a> is more useful in late July 2026 than the older June call notices because it shows the current application window and the paperwork the portal expects. As of <strong>Monday, July 27, 2026</strong>, that page lists the second 2026 call window as <strong>June 15, 2026 through September 15, 2026</strong>.</p>

<p>For Rex readers, that shifts the question. This is no longer mainly a deadline-watch item. It is a workflow question for foreign publishers, scouts, and rights managers: <strong>can you package the rights deal, the translator, and the market plan cleanly enough to survive the portal without correction loops?</strong></p>

<h2 class="mt-10 mb-4 text-2xl font-bold text-gray-900">What the programme covers now</h2>

<p>The current programme page still frames ©POLAND as outward-translation infrastructure, not as a general cultural-promotion note. It says the scheme is for foreign and Polish publishers publishing Polish books in other languages, including <strong>print, ebook, and audiobook editions distributed outside Poland</strong>.</p>

<p>That matters because the cost support is broader than many translation-fund headlines suggest. The programme materials say support can include:</p>

<ul class="mb-6 list-disc pl-6 space-y-2">
  <li><strong>Translation costs</strong> for the work itself.</li>
  <li><strong>Copyright-license costs</strong> tied to the rights agreement.</li>
  <li><strong>Print costs</strong> for children's books, comics, graphic novels, and other richly illustrated works.</li>
</ul>

<p>The same live page identifies <strong>Scandinavian languages and Italian</strong> as 2026 priorities. That should be read as a selection signal, not as a claim that other target languages are excluded.</p>

<h2 class="mt-10 mb-4 text-2xl font-bold text-gray-900">The portal is better, but it is less forgiving of vague prep</h2>

<p>The Book Institute's related <a href="https://booksfrompoland.com/first-call-of-the-poland-translation-programme-2026/">Books From Poland notice about the 2026 IT system</a> explains what changed in practical terms. Applicants now create an account, complete the application inside the portal, and can receive notifications when corrections or additional information are needed.</p>

<p>That is an improvement, but it also means weak preparation is easier to spot. The same notice says the form now uses an <strong>author and translator database</strong>, adds a short <strong>motivation field</strong> explaining why the publisher wants to publish the book, and asks for extra justification when the project is a reprint.</p>

<p>In other words, the portal does not merely collect attachments. It forces the publisher to show that the project already has editorial intent, translator clarity, and basic market logic.</p>

<h2 class="mt-10 mb-4 text-2xl font-bold text-gray-900">What publishers should have ready before opening the form</h2>

<p>The fastest way to turn this into avoidable back-and-forth is to treat the application as the beginning of the project. The public programme materials point the other way. Before logging in, a serious applicant should already have:</p>

<ol class="mb-6 list-decimal pl-6 space-y-2">
  <li><strong>A signed rights contract.</strong> The programme page asks for a signed contract with the copyright owner, not a half-settled rights conversation.</li>
  <li><strong>A signed translator contract.</strong> The translator is not a placeholder field. The application expects a real translator arrangement.</li>
  <li><strong>Publisher and title information that will survive review.</strong> The profile, catalogue material, title data, and translator details need to match the actual project.</li>
  <li><strong>Cost detail that makes commercial sense.</strong> Translation, rights, and print-cost figures should line up with the signed paperwork and the edition type.</li>
  <li><strong>A promotion and distribution plan.</strong> The Institute explicitly asks how the book will be promoted and distributed, which means the panel is reading for market seriousness, not only literary merit.</li>
  <li><strong>A short motivation that sounds like a publisher, not a brochure.</strong> The new field is an argument for why this book belongs in that market.</li>
</ol>

<p>That package is why the scheme works best for projects that are already real. If the rights are still drifting, the translator is not chosen, or the target-market plan is still generic, the portal will expose that quickly.</p>

<h2 class="mt-10 mb-4 text-2xl font-bold text-gray-900">Why the new window changes the practical advice</h2>

<p>Rex already covered the broader ©POLAND second-call funding lane in June. The more useful July update is narrower. If the live programme page now keeps the window open until <strong>September 15, 2026</strong>, the pressure point is no longer simply "file fast." It is <strong>use the extra time to tighten the package instead of filing a mess earlier</strong>.</p>

<p>That is especially relevant for small publishers working across languages and formats. A few additional weeks only help if they are used to clean up contracts, lock translator details, check print-cost eligibility for illustrated categories, and write a distribution plan that matches the target territory.</p>

<p>The older Books From Poland post is still useful for the portal mechanics, but it is not the safer place to anchor the live deadline. For deadline-sensitive decisions, the current Book Institute programme page should carry more weight than a dated news post.</p>

<h2 class="mt-10 mb-4 text-2xl font-bold text-gray-900">The practical takeaway</h2>

<p>The best use of Poland's 2026 translation portal is not to discover whether a project exists. It is to <strong>package a project that is already contractually and commercially coherent</strong>. The rights agreement, translator contract, cost logic, and market plan should already be strong enough that the portal simply records them.</p>

<p>If that foundation is in place, the programme can support a real foreign-language edition workflow across translation, rights, and in some cases print. If it is not, the new portal will mostly help the panel identify what is missing.</p>
