<p>As of <strong>Tuesday, August 25, 2026</strong>, the National Centre for Writing's <a href="https://nationalcentreforwriting.org.uk/writing-hub/apply-now-writers-residency-in-norwich-with-lci/">live residency call</a> says applications are open for either <strong>a Lithuanian author</strong> or <strong>a collaborating author-translator team</strong> to spend <strong>February 1 to 26, 2027</strong> in Norwich. The Lithuanian Cultural Institute posted the same call on <a href="https://lithuanianculture.lt/kulturos-naujienos/2026/08/25/kvietimas-teikti-paraiskas-i-rezidencija-nacionaliniame-rasymo-centre-noridze-jungtineje-karalysteje/">August 25, 2026</a>, which matters because it confirms this is a current open opportunity on both sides of the partnership. For Rex readers, though, the useful question is not whether the residency sounds attractive. It is whether the project packet is already clear enough to survive the <strong>Monday, October 19, 2026</strong> deadline.</p>

<p>This is not a generic culture-news item. NCW says authors must be active in the Lithuanian literary scene and have at least <strong>one book published in Lithuanian</strong>, with <strong>self-published books excluded</strong> for this call. If the application is joint, NCW says the translator should already have at least <strong>one book-length translation published in English</strong>. That makes the residency useful at a specific stage of the workflow: late enough that the work and partners are real, but early enough that publisher interest, translation development, and English-language planning can still be shaped together.</p>

<h2 class="mt-10 mb-4 text-2xl font-bold text-gray-900">The solo-author route and the joint author-translator route are not the same packet</h2>

<p>That distinction is the center of the story. NCW is offering one residency structure, but not one identical application logic for everyone. A solo author has to prove literary-track eligibility and explain why the residency timing matters now. A joint author-translator team has to do that <strong>and</strong> show that the collaboration itself is developed enough to justify a shared month in Norwich.</p>

<p>The same NCW page says joint applicants should submit <strong>a collaborative project outline</strong> and explain why they would benefit from the residency at this particular moment. That requirement turns the call into more than a simple mobility grant. It asks whether the author and translator already know what project they will advance, what kind of English-language development they need, and why concentrated time together is commercially or editorially useful now.</p>

<h2 class="mt-10 mb-4 text-2xl font-bold text-gray-900">Publisher interest matters here even though a publishing contract is not required</h2>

<p>One detail on the NCW page deserves careful reading. The call says author-translator teams <strong>do not need to have been commissioned to publish a book together</strong>, but it also says applicants should include <strong>information about any publisher interest</strong> in the project, and that existing commissioning interest will be treated as <strong>an advantage</strong>.</p>

<p>That is a practical filter, not a contradiction. The residency is still open to projects that have not yet closed a final publishing deal. But NCW is clearly signaling that the strongest packets will show more than artistic enthusiasm. A translator, scout, or rights-facing team should be able to point to some real market path, even if that path is still early. If there is no project definition, no likely English-language route, and no credible publisher signal at all, the application may be too early rather than merely incomplete.</p>

<h2 class="mt-10 mb-4 text-2xl font-bold text-gray-900">The support is real, but it only helps if the residency month already solves a real problem</h2>

<p>NCW says the resident or residents will receive <strong>free accommodation at Dragon Hall cottage</strong>, <strong>one roundtrip economy flight each</strong>, <strong>reimbursed local travel</strong>, and a <strong>GBP 350 weekly stipend</strong>, or <strong>GBP 1,400 total</strong>, toward living expenses. The Lithuanian Cultural Institute's same-day post also points to <strong>up to two mentoring sessions with an experienced literary translator</strong> and a networking session with local writers and publishing professionals.</p>

<p>That package is meaningful, but readers should keep the order of operations straight. Mentoring and networking are most useful when the manuscript, sample, and collaboration logic already exist. Residency time can sharpen a translation or move an English-language plan forward. It does not replace the earlier work of deciding which Lithuanian book is in play, which translator relationship is credible, and what kind of publisher interest can honestly be shown in the application.</p>

<h2 class="mt-10 mb-4 text-2xl font-bold text-gray-900">What to assemble before Monday, October 19, 2026</h2>

<ol class="mb-6 list-decimal pl-6 space-y-2">
  <li><strong>Check the route first.</strong> Decide whether this is a solo-author application or a joint author-translator application, because the evidence burden is different.</li>
  <li><strong>Verify the author eligibility exactly.</strong> NCW says the author must be active in the Lithuanian literary scene and have at least one non-self-published book in Lithuanian.</li>
  <li><strong>Verify the translator track honestly.</strong> Joint applications should use a translator who already has at least one book-length translation published in English.</li>
  <li><strong>Write the project outline before polishing the pitch.</strong> NCW asks for CVs, short biographical statements, and a collaborative project outline where relevant.</li>
  <li><strong>State the publisher signal precisely.</strong> Do not invent certainty. If there is publisher interest, describe it accurately. If there is no publisher contract yet, keep that clear.</li>
  <li><strong>Use the residency to solve a defined next-step problem.</strong> The strongest packet will explain why February 2027 in Norwich is the right moment for this project, not just why residencies are generally desirable.</li>
</ol>
