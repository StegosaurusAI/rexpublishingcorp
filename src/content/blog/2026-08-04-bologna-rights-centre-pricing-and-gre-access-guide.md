<p>As of <strong>Friday, August 14, 2026</strong>, Bologna Children's Book Fair's live <a href="https://www.bolognachildrensbookfair.com/en/exhibitors/interested-in-exhibiting/exhibiting-at-the-rights-centre/15999.html">Rights Centre page</a> puts a clearer price on 2027 planning than many smaller rights teams had a few weeks ago. The organiser says a <strong>table costs 940 euros before October 31, 2026 and 985 euros from November 1, 2026</strong>, while an <strong>office costs 1,250 euros before October 31 and 1,320 euros from November 1</strong>, with VAT added where due. The useful decision for Rex readers is not whether Bologna is prestigious. It is whether a team will use both the in-person meeting base and the bundled <strong>Global Rights Exchange</strong>, or GRE, enough to justify booking before the price change.</p>

<p>This matters because Bologna is no longer asking buyers to plan around vague fair visibility alone. The same page says the participation fee includes <strong>registration on the Global Rights Exchange platform</strong>, an exhibitor pass, parking, vouchers, Wi-Fi, compulsory insurance, and catalogue inclusion. That turns the Rights Centre from a pure on-site cost into a combined access-and-workflow purchase.</p>

<h2 class="mt-10 mb-4 text-2xl font-bold text-gray-900">The first planning split is table versus office, not yes versus no</h2>

<p>The Rights Centre page says the space is open to <strong>both children's and adult agents</strong>, and that exhibiting publishers across BCBF and BolognaBookPlus can buy tables or offices there. That is a narrower and more useful framing than the general claim that "Bologna is important for rights."</p>

<p>For many small agencies, scouts, and rights-facing presses, the realistic choice is not between taking a large stand somewhere else and buying a Bologna office here. It is between a <strong>lower-cost table</strong> that gives one person a dedicated meeting base and a <strong>higher-cost office</strong> that makes sense only if privacy, heavier scheduling, or repeated partner meetings justify the jump. Bologna also says each option is <strong>valid for one person</strong> and that additional colleagues require a <strong>490-euro co-member fee</strong>. That extra line matters because a cheap-looking base price can stop looking cheap once multiple staff are added.</p>

<h2 class="mt-10 mb-4 text-2xl font-bold text-gray-900">GRE is the part that can make the package more than four fair days</h2>

<p>Bologna's <a href="https://www.bolognachildrensbookfair.com/en/exhibitors/bologna-global-rights-exchange/10135.html">Global Rights Exchange page</a> describes GRE as a virtual marketplace for international rights, distribution, and licensing. The organiser says it hosts about <strong>22,000 titles</strong> and more than <strong>200 properties</strong>, and that users can upload content, search trends, chat in their own language, and schedule meetings.</p>

<p>That year-round layer is the real business case behind the Rights Centre pricing. BolognaBookPlus's <a href="https://www.bolognachildrensbookfair.com/en/about/bolognabookplus/events-and-training-bolognabookplus/rights-bolognabookplus/11381.html">Rights at BolognaBookPlus page</a> says GRE helps exhibitors and visitors continue to <strong>showcase, discover, and trade rights across the world</strong>, with support that extends across the year rather than only during fair week. If a team will actually load titles, keep rights information current, and use the platform to line up meetings, the fee looks different from a short-lived floor cost. If it will not, then the package becomes much harder to defend.</p>

<h2 class="mt-10 mb-4 text-2xl font-bold text-gray-900">Do not confuse bundled access with guaranteed deal flow</h2>

<p>The organiser's pages make GRE sound useful, and it likely is. But none of them say that paying for a table or office guarantees quality meetings, faster rights sales, or stronger catalogue discovery by itself. The cleaner reading is operational. Bologna is offering a <strong>dedicated meeting space plus a rights-platform workflow</strong>. Teams still have to do the hard part: clear rights metadata, usable sample material, appointment discipline, and follow-up.</p>

<p>That is especially important for smaller teams tempted to buy access first and work out the catalogue later. GRE can support a rights process. It does not replace one. If the titles are not ready to present, if territorial rights are fuzzy, or if nobody owns outbound follow-up, the cheaper pre-November rate is still wasted money.</p>

<h2 class="mt-10 mb-4 text-2xl font-bold text-gray-900">The October 31 cutoff matters because the next useful question is readiness, not urgency theater</h2>

<p>The live Rights Centre page creates a real budgeting deadline: <strong>October 31, 2026</strong> is the last day before the price step-up. That does not mean every rights team should rush to book now. It means teams that were already likely to attend have a practical window to decide whether the Rights Centre format fits their 2027 plan better than a looser meeting strategy.</p>

<p>For some readers, the honest answer will still be no. A team with a thin list, limited international follow-up capacity, or only a handful of exploratory conversations may not need a Rights Centre base. But a team already preparing rights meetings for spring 2027 can now price the decision with more confidence than before.</p>

<h2 class="mt-10 mb-4 text-2xl font-bold text-gray-900">What to check before November 1, 2026</h2>

<ol class="mb-6 list-decimal pl-6 space-y-2">
  <li><strong>Choose the space honestly.</strong> A table works if one person mainly needs a reliable meeting base. An office is harder to justify unless privacy or heavier scheduling is real.</li>
  <li><strong>Count heads early.</strong> Bologna says extra colleagues trigger a 490-euro co-member fee, so the real budget is not only the headline table or office price.</li>
  <li><strong>Decide whether GRE will be used year-round.</strong> The platform value appears only if the team will maintain listings, search actively, and schedule follow-up meetings.</li>
  <li><strong>Separate workflow value from fair mythology.</strong> Buy the package for meetings and rights operations, not because Bologna feels mandatory in the abstract.</li>
  <li><strong>Freeze the catalogue prep before you book.</strong> Bundled access is more valuable when title material, rights status, and outreach ownership are already clear.</li>
</ol>
