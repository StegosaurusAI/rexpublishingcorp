A translation grant is not money available when the translator signs. For a publisher planning a printed translation with FILI support, the useful task is to connect the application file to the later payment file—and budget the interval between them.

This guide follows that print-edition sequence. It is for authors, translators and rights holders coordinating with the publisher, not a tested application walkthrough or a recommendation of particular books.

## Before filing: put the publisher and documents together

[FILI's foreign-publisher rules](https://fili.fi/en/grants/tukimuodot/kaannos-ja-painatustuki-ulkomaisille/) require the publisher outside Finland to have acquired translation rights and signed a translator agreement before applying. Eligible originals were published in Finland, in Finnish, Finland-Swedish or Sámi; listed categories include fiction, general nonfiction, children's books and comics.

The application needs the signed translator agreement and a translator CV or Kääntöpiiri profile. A relevant printing-grant request also needs a printing quote. Printing support is limited to comics, graphic novels and children's picture books and requires a simultaneous translation-grant application. Other translation funding affects FILI's award; Creative Europe-funded books are excluded. [Check the programme rules](https://fili.fi/en/grants/tukimuodot/kaannos-ja-painatustuki-ulkomaisille/).

As checked October 27, the rules give **November 1, 2026** as this round's closing date. No exact cutoff hour is established here. Submit before publication; this date does not establish portal availability or remaining funds.

## Assessment is a separate gate

[FILI's evaluation guidance](https://fili.fi/en/grants/evaluation-and-decisions/) describes advisory-board assessment, not automatic payment for a complete form. Criteria include professional publishing activity, marketing and distribution, and translator skills. Missing attachments, unclear licensing and insufficient funds are among the reasons applications fail.

Our planning recommendation: keep two budget versions, one without support and one reflecting only an actual award. Assign a person to update the production calendar when the decision arrives. A submitted application should not become an assumed receivable in the team's working cash plan.

## Publication and payment need their own file

The [recipient instructions](https://fili.fi/en/grants/for-grant-recipients/) require the full contracted translator fee regardless of the grant outcome. Payment evidence includes a bank statement proving translator payment and generally a signed funding agreement, with the stated exception for Nordic Council of Ministers-funded grants.

For this print route, disbursement requires the book to reach the SKS library and a payment request with mandatory attachments in FILI's system. Check current delivery instructions rather than copying an old address. FILI also says not to print before grant decisions: the book needs the required acknowledgement and logo. Credit the translator in the book and communications. [Read the publication and payment requirements](https://fili.fi/en/grants/for-grant-recipients/).

That sequence makes the practical question: **who covers the translator and production bills before grant cash arrives?** A possible award does not answer it.

## Build one handoff record, not scattered reminders

The table below is Rex's suggested responsibility record, not a FILI form. The roles are proposals for your team; the underlying requirements are linked above.

| Handoff | Suggested owner | Record to keep together |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Rights and edition | Publisher's rights lead | Rights agreement, edition language and planned publication date |
| Translator file | Translation editor | Signed agreement, qualifications and agreed fee |
| Application | Publisher's grant lead | Submitted version, attachments and confirmation, if received |
| Decision and print approval | Production editor | Actual decision and checked acknowledgement/credit proof |
| Payment evidence | Finance lead | Translator-payment proof, applicable funding agreement and request record |
| Printed-copy delivery | Production coordinator | Dispatch details and delivery follow-up |

Add an owner, target date and unresolved-question column to your project copy. Ask each owner to mark the exact version they checked. This avoids a common handoff problem: finance working from a draft budget while production works from a later contract.

Before committing the print schedule, hold a short readiness review. Can the team locate the signed documents, explain the no-award budget and name the person responsible for the payment request? If not, resolve those gaps rather than treating the approaching round close as a reason to skip them.

## Keep the scope narrow

This is a print-edition checklist. It does not establish ebook-only fulfilment, regional funding guarantees or a successful filing. FILI's eligibility, evaluation and payment pages are separate evidence buckets from the programme operator—not independent market corroboration. No award, remaining budget or guaranteed decision date is implied.
